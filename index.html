ERP Implementation Methodology & Process Gap Analysis

8-step sequential methodology ('as-is' to 'to-be') and interactive functional gap classification register.

Slides 04 – 05
Sequential Discipline (Slide 04): This 8-step sequence ensures that technology choices follow an understanding of business needs and target processes—never merely automating inefficient practices.
Process Mapping & Gap Analysis (Slide 05): Distinguish process changes from genuine system limitations. Classify gaps by effort: Small (project team resolves), Extensive, or Not Feasible (escalated to Steering Committee).

1List business functions

Identify major functions (procurement, production, sales) to establish which processes must be understood during ERP design.

2Map process time, decision points & departments

Record how long work takes, where decisions occur and which departments or locations participate to reveal delays and complexity.

3Record information flows & reporting points

Trace how information moves between people and departments and where reports are produced to uncover duplication and bottlenecks.

4Benchmark cost, quality, lead time & service

Compare current performance with desired or industry benchmarks to set measurable improvement goals.

5Map redesigned processes to standard ERP

Match future workflows with functions already available in the ERP package. Standard features avoid unnecessary development.

6–8Record, classify & escalate gaps

Document unmet requirements as gaps; classify by effort (Small, Extensive, Not Feasible). Project team resolves minor gaps; Steering Committee decides on major resources or process changes.
